Market Terminology
#1 Share / Stock
A Share represents a unit of ownership in a company. When you buy a share, you become a partial owner of that company. "Stock" generally refers to the ownership certificate of any company.
EXAMPLE If you buy 1 share of Reliance Industries for ₹2,500, you own a tiny fraction of Reliance. If the company value goes up, your share price goes up.
#2 Sensex & Nifty
These are benchmark indices that represent the health of the Indian Stock Market.
Sensex: Tracks the top 30 companies on the BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange).
Nifty 50: Tracks the top 50 companies on the NSE (National Stock Exchange).
EXAMPLE If Nifty goes up by 1%, it generally means the top 50 major Indian companies performed well that day.
#3 Bull vs Bear Market
Market sentiments describing the trend of stock prices.
Bull Market: Prices are rising, and investor confidence is high.
Bear Market: Prices are falling (usually >20%), and sentiment is pessimistic.
EXAMPLE "The market is bullish" means everyone expects stocks to go up. "The market is bearish" means people are selling in fear.
#4 Dividend
A portion of the company's profit distributed to its shareholders. Not all companies pay dividends; some reinvest profits back into growth.
EXAMPLE ITC declares a dividend of ₹5 per share. If you hold 100 shares, you receive ₹500 directly in your bank account.
#5 IPO (Initial Public Offering)
The process by which a private company offers shares to the public for the first time to raise capital. This is when a company lists on the stock exchange.
EXAMPLE When Zomato listed on the stock market in 2021, it launched an IPO where public investors could buy shares before they started trading.
#6 Demat Account
Short for "Dematerialized Account". It is like a bank account but for shares. It holds your shares in electronic format. You need one to trade in India.
EXAMPLE When you buy shares via Zerodha or Upstox, the shares are digitally stored in your CDSL/NSDL Demat account.
#7 Intraday vs Delivery
Intraday: Buying and selling a stock on the same day. No shares enter your Demat account.
Delivery: Buying a stock and holding it for more than one day. Shares are delivered to your Demat account (T+1 day).
EXAMPLE Buying Tata Motors at 10 AM and selling at 3 PM is Intraday. Buying it and selling it next week is Delivery.
#8 Market Capitalization (Market Cap)
The total market value of a company's outstanding shares. It determines the size of the company.
Formula: Current Share Price × Total Number of Shares.
EXAMPLE Reliance is a "Large Cap" company (High value). A new small company might be "Small Cap" (Higher risk, higher growth potential).
#9 P/E Ratio
Price-to-Earnings Ratio. It measures how expensive a stock is relative to its earnings.
Formula: Share Price / Earnings Per Share (EPS).
EXAMPLE A P/E of 20 means investors are willing to pay ₹20 for every ₹1 of profit the company makes. High P/E usually means high growth expectations.
#10 Portfolio
A collection of financial investments like stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and cash held by an individual or institution.
EXAMPLE Your portfolio might consist of 50% IT stocks (TCS, Infy), 30% Bank stocks (HDFC, SBI), and 20% Gold.
#11 Volatility
A measure of how much a stock's price swings up and down. High volatility means high risk but potential for high quick returns.
EXAMPLE Blue-chip stocks like HUL have low volatility (stable). Crypto or Small-cap stocks often have high volatility (wild price swings).
#12 Stop Loss
An order placed to sell a security when it reaches a certain price. It is used to limit an investor's loss on a position.
EXAMPLE You buy a stock at ₹100. You set a Stop Loss at ₹95. If the price falls to ₹95, it automatically sells to prevent you from losing more if it crashes to ₹80.
